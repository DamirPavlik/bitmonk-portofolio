## The Death of Stack Overflow

I still remember coding late at night on my Windows machine. XAMPP running, PHP humming, autocomplete on, Stack Overflow open in the next tab. Life was good.

A lot of us learned to build for the web there, but Stack Overflow had answers for almost everything. Whatever error you pasted into Google, a Stack Overflow thread was usually the first result.

At its peak, the site got more than 207,000 new questions a month. Today it gets fewer than 1,000. And back then every one of those questions came from a real person, stuck on something, usually fighting with React.

## Then came ChatGPT

In November 2022, ChatGPT launched, and it raised an obvious question: why dig through old threads when I can just ask?

Stack Overflow pushed back. In December 2022 it banned answers generated by ChatGPT. The fight over AI didn't end there. In mid-2023 the moderators went on strike over the company's AI policy, and around the same time the site paused its public data dumps.

The layoffs came next: about 10% of staff in May 2023, then nearly 30% more in October.

It's hard to imagine now, but people used to chase Stack Overflow fame. A strong profile with plenty of reputation and a few legendary answers could actually help you land a job.

## 2024: selling the data

Stack Overflow belongs to Prosus, a giant tech investor, and big investors want the most out of every asset. So the content itself became the product.

In February 2024, Google signed an OverflowAPI deal. In May, OpenAI followed. The questions and answers we wrote for free were now training AI. Some users protested by deleting their posts, and they got suspended for it.

## Ghost town

The numbers speak for themselves:

| Month             | New questions |
| ----------------- | ------------- |
| March 2014 (peak) | 207,168       |
| September 2025    | 5,075         |
| September 2026    | 966           |

That is a drop of 99.5% from the peak. Prosus has written down about $1.5 billion, roughly 87% of what it paid, and Stack Overflow is now valued at around $230 million.

## 2026: still alive, kind of

Here is the twist: in 2026 Stack Overflow posted its first operating profit, $1 million on $129 million in revenue. The money comes from two places:

- **Stack Internal**, private Q&A for companies
- **Stack Overflow for Agents**, launched in 2026, where bots post for other bots

The site that taught a generation of developers how to code is still standing. The people asking the questions are just gone.
